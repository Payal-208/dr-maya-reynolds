THERAPY WITH DR. MAYA REYNOLDS, PSYD

ABOUT DR. MAYA REYNOLDS, PSYD

A licensed clinical psychologist in Santa Monica, California.

Dr. Maya Reynolds offers therapy for adults who feel overwhelmed by anxiety, stress, or the lingering effects of past experiences. Many of the people she works with are high-achieving, thoughtful, and self-aware—but internally feel exhausted, stuck in overthinking, or emotionally on edge.

Her work often focuses on anxiety, panic, trauma, and burnout, including single-incident trauma and more complex, long-standing patterns. She also supports entrepreneurs, creatives, and professionals navigating burnout, perfectionism, and high internal pressure after years of pushing through stress.

Dr. Reynolds takes a warm, collaborative, and grounded approach, integrating cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), EMDR, mindfulness-based practices, and body-oriented techniques. Sessions are structured enough to feel supportive, with space for reflection and depth. She offers in-person therapy from her Santa Monica office and secure telehealth sessions for clients located in California.

My goal is not just symptom relief, but helping clients develop insight, resilience, and a stronger relationship with themselves over time.

HOW THERAPY WORKS

Practical support, with room to understand the deeper story.

A WARM, COLLABORATIVE, AND GROUNDED SPACE FOR ADULTS WHO HAVE BEEN CARRYING A LOT.

Dr. Reynolds combines evidence-based methods—including cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), EMDR, mindfulness-based practices, and body-oriented techniques—to support both the emotional and physiological sides of what you are experiencing. Sessions are structured enough to feel supportive while leaving room for reflection and depth. Therapy is paced with care, especially when working with trauma, and focuses on safety, stabilization, and skills you can use in daily life.

Therapy is an active, collaborative process. Together, you and Dr. Reynolds can explore patterns, make room for difficult emotions, and practice tools that support you in everyday life. The goal is not only symptom relief, but also greater insight, resilience, and a stronger relationship with yourself over time.

FAQS

Questions about therapy in Santa Monica?

Starting therapy is a personal decision. Here are answers to common questions about working with Dr. Reynolds, in person or by secure telehealth.

Get in Touch
Where is Dr. Reynolds’ office, and is telehealth available?

Dr. Reynolds offers in-person therapy from her Santa Monica office at 123th Street 45 W, Santa Monica, CA 90401. Secure telehealth sessions are available for clients located in California.

Who does Dr. Reynolds work with?

She works with adults who feel overwhelmed by anxiety, stress, or the lingering effects of past experiences. Many clients are high-achieving, thoughtful, and self-aware, and include entrepreneurs, creatives, and professionals who feel disconnected from themselves after years of pushing through stress.

What can I expect in therapy sessions?

Dr. Reynolds takes a warm, collaborative, and grounded approach. Sessions are structured enough to feel supportive, while leaving space for reflection and depth. Therapy is an active process: together you explore patterns, make room for difficult emotions, and practice tools you can use in daily life.

What methods does Dr. Reynolds use?

She integrates evidence-based methods, including cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), EMDR, mindfulness-based practices, and body-oriented techniques, to address both the emotional and physiological sides of what you are experiencing.

How does Dr. Reynolds approach trauma therapy?

Trauma work is paced carefully, with an emphasis on safety, stabilization, and helping you feel more regulated in your daily life, not just during sessions. She works with single-incident trauma as well as more complex, long-standing patterns that may stem from childhood, relationships, or chronic stress.

I look like I’m managing. Is therapy still for me?

Many of Dr. Reynolds’ clients feel “functional” on the outside while quietly struggling with constant worry, tension in the body, difficulty sleeping, or a sense of always bracing for something to go wrong. Therapy can be a space to slow down, reconnect, and develop more sustainable ways of living and working.
